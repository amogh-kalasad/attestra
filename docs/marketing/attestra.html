✦ The problem

Security testing produces evidence. Audits need proof.

Teams already run scanners and pentests. The hard part is turning scattered results into prioritized work and audit-ready attestation.

⧉

Findings scattered across tools

ZAP, Nuclei, Trivy, Semgrep and other SARIF producers each ship their own formats. The same issue often appears more than once.

⇅

Hard to prioritize

CVSS alone does not reflect exploitability in the wild or how critical the affected asset is to the business.

▦

Compliance mapping is manual

Linking findings to PCI DSS, ISO 27001 or SOC 2 requirements is slow, inconsistent, and difficult to defend in audit.

✎

Reporting takes too long

Technical detail for testers, executive summaries for leadership, and framework evidence packs for assessors are rebuilt by hand.

⌀

Scope and authorization gaps

Without a clear signed authorization and in-scope assets, imports and retests are hard to justify under ISO A.8.34-style controls.

⛓

Weak evidence integrity

Stakeholders need to know raw imports, evidence files and reports have not been silently altered after the engagement.

✦ The Attestra solution

Import. Normalize. Attest.

Attestra is an import-and-report VAPT evidence platform. Testers run scanners under client authorization; Attestra turns those results into ranked findings, proposed control status, and signed reports.

1

Engage

Create tenant & engagement with target frameworks

2

3

Add assets with criticality, exposure & PCI CDE flags

4

6

7

Attestra does not attack targets on its own. Result files are imported (or produced by scoped runner dispatch under engagement authorization), then treated as evidence.

✦ Features & capabilities

Built for evidence you can defend

Capabilities below reflect implemented Attestra functionality — not a wishlist.

Engagement & authorization

Engagements

Tenants and engagements for VA, PT, VAPT, retest or continuous work, with test windows and selected frameworks.

Benefit: one place for client context and audit cycle scope.

Authorization gate

Signed authorization is hashed and stored write-once. Imports are refused when no currently valid authorization exists.

Benefit: clear proof that testing stayed inside authorized bounds.

Scoped assets

Record targets with criticality (1–5), internet/internal exposure, and PCI CDE flags for framework-aware evaluation.

Benefit: risk and PCI scoping follow the asset, not the spreadsheet.

Import & findings

Multi-tool import

OWASP ZAP JSON, Nuclei JSONL, Trivy JSON (vulns & misconfigs), and any SARIF 2.1 (Semgrep, CodeQL, Checkov, Gitleaks and others).

Benefit: keep the tools your team trusts; Attestra normalizes the output.

Fingerprint de-duplication

One finding schema keyed on CWE / CVE / location. Cross-tool and cross-run duplicates merge by fingerprint.

Benefit: less noise, clearer remediation queues.

Risk prioritization

Risk combines CVSS, FIRST EPSS, CISA KEV, asset criticality and exposure. Reports still show CVSS as the severity of record.

Benefit: work the issues that matter first.

Finding lifecycle

Statuses from new through triaged, validated, fixed and retest_passed — plus false_positive and risk_accepted (reason required). Fixed findings that reappear reopen.

Benefit: remediation progress is explicit and auditable.

SLA tracking

Due dates such as critical findings within 30 days (aligned to PCI 6.3.3 expectations), with ageing and SLA breach reporting.

Benefit: overdue risk is visible before audit day.

Evidence store

Uploaded artifacts are masked where needed, SHA-256 hashed, content-addressed and stored write-once.

Benefit: every claim can point back to a hashed artifact.

Compliance & reporting

Hub-and-spoke mapping

Findings map once to a control objective via CWE, then fan out to PCI DSS 4.0.1, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and SOC 2.

Benefit: add frameworks through the mapping library, not one-off scripts.

Propose, then confirm

The engine proposes control status; a named assessor confirms with rationale. No exceptions is distinct from met.

Benefit: absence of findings is never mistaken for proof a control is in place.

Signed reports

Versioned HTML reports frozen by SHA-256, moved through submit → QA → sign, then signed with Ed25519 (HMAC fallback).

Benefit: deliverables that can be verified after they leave the platform.

Operations & interfaces

Web, API & CLI

Server-rendered dashboard, REST API with OpenAPI docs, and a CLI that share the same business rules and audit trail.

Benefit: humans and automation use one trusted path.

Hash-chained audit log

Append-only audit events with tamper verification for business writes across the engagement lifecycle.

Benefit: reconstruct who did what, and prove the chain still holds.

Portal & Trust Center

Client portal and auditor views for engagement visibility, plus a public Trust Center surface for controlled external access.

Benefit: share status without emailing sensitive binders.

✦ How it fits

Where Attestra sits in your workflow

Attestra coordinates evidence around testing you already perform — it does not replace remediation owners or the assessor’s judgment.

Before testing

Create the engagement, upload the signed authorization, and register in-scope assets (including CDE flags when PCI is in play).

During testing

Run scanners under that authorization. Import result files (or use scoped runner dispatch). Enrich with KEV/EPSS and triage findings with named validation.

After testing

Evaluate compliance proposals, confirm controls as assessor, generate reports, complete four-eyes QA and signing, then share portal or Trust Center access as needed.

Human review stays required

False positives, risk acceptance, control confirmation and report QA/sign-off are deliberate human steps — not silent automation.
